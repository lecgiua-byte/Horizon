### [Google Removes MV2 Extensions, Including uBlock Origin, from Chrome Web Store](https://webiterate.dev/google-removed-extensions-ublock-origin-108/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google has removed all Manifest V2 \(MV2\) extensions from the Chrome Web Store, including popular ad-blocker uBlock Origin. This forces users to switch to MV3-compliant alternatives or migrate to other browsers like Firefox, which still supports MV2. The move affects millions of users and signals a major shift in the browser extension ecosystem.

hackernews · twapi · Aug 31, 21:10 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49514878)

**「Background」** Google Chrome extensions are built on a framework called &\#x27;Manifest&\#x27;, which defines what capabilities an extension can access. For years, extensions used Manifest V2 \(MV2\), which allowed powerful features like blocking network requests before they reach the browser. This made MV2 ideal for ad blockers such as uBlock Origin, which intercepts and blocks ads and trackers at the network level.

In 2019, Google announced a transition to Manifest V3 \(MV3\), a new framework that restricts these capabilities. MV3 limits the use of the &\#x27;webRequest&\#x27; API, which MV2 ad blockers relied on, and replaces it with a less flexible &\#x27;declarativeNetRequest&\#x27; API. Google stated that MV3 improves security, privacy, and performance, but critics argue it also reduces the effectiveness of ad blockers and gives Google more control over what extensions can do.

Over the past few years, Google has been gradually phasing out MV2 extensions. In 2024, Chrome began disabling MV2 extensions for users, and by early 2025, Google started removing them from the Chrome Web Store entirely. uBlock Origin, one of the most popular ad blockers with millions of users, was among those removed because it does not comply with MV3. Google recommends users switch to MV3-compliant alternatives like uBlock Origin Lite, which has reduced capabilities.

This move is part of a broader industry trend where browser vendors are tightening extension policies, but it is particularly significant because Chrome holds a dominant share of the browser market \(around 65% globally\). The removal affects a large user base and has sparked frustration among users who rely on ad blocking for privacy, security, and a cleaner browsing experience.

**「Commercial Signal」** Google&\#x27;s removal of Manifest V2 extensions like uBlock Origin from the Chrome Web Store creates immediate, observable demand for ad-blocking alternatives that work under the new Manifest V3 rules. Millions of Chrome users who relied on uBlock Origin now need a replacement, and many are frustrated by the change. This is a real pain point, not a hypothetical one.

Who has this problem? Chrome users who want effective ad and tracker blocking, especially those who used uBlock Origin. This includes privacy-conscious individuals, families concerned about malicious ads \(as one commenter noted, ad blocking is a safety issue for less tech-savvy relatives\), and businesses that want to reduce distractions and protect employees from malvertising.

Who is willing to pay? Some users will pay for a reliable ad blocker, especially if it offers additional features like VPN or anti-tracking. The market already includes paid options like Total Adblock, Surfshark, and NordVPN Threat Protection, which bundle ad blocking with other privacy tools. However, many users expect free ad blockers, so the willingness to pay may be limited unless the product clearly solves a bigger problem.

Which companies may benefit? Existing MV3-compliant ad blockers like AdBlock Plus, AdBlock, and Total Adblock could gain users. Privacy-focused browsers like Firefox and Brave may see increased adoption as users migrate away from Chrome. Companies offering VPNs with built-in ad blocking \(e.g., Surfshark, NordVPN\) could also benefit. Conversely, Google&\#x27;s own ad revenue may increase if ad blocking becomes less effective, but that&\#x27;s a separate concern.

Which business models are threatened? The traditional free ad-blocker model that relies on donations or optional paid tiers may struggle if MV3 limits functionality. Also, any extension that depended on MV2&\#x27;s powerful APIs is now obsolete, so developers of those extensions must adapt or lose their user base.

Is there a product opportunity? Yes, several:
\- Develop a high-quality MV3-compliant ad blocker that is transparent about its whitelisting policies \(unlike AdBlock Plus and AdBlock, which have paid whitelists on by default\). Users are increasingly wary of ad blockers that let some ads through, so a truly independent, open-source option could gain trust.
\- Create a migration tool that helps users switch from Chrome to Firefox or another browser, preserving their bookmarks, passwords, and settings. This is a practical need for the many users who are considering switching.
\- Offer a consulting or installation service for less technical users, especially older adults, to set up safe browsing with ad blocking and anti-malware protection. This could be a local service or a remote support offering.

Could a small team or individual participate? Yes. Building a simple MV3 ad blocker is feasible for a small developer team, though competing with established players is hard. A migration tool is simpler and could be a weekend project. The consulting service requires no technical expertise beyond knowing how to configure browsers and extensions.

Could a business model be transferred from another country? Yes, the concept of paid privacy bundles \(VPN + ad blocking\) is already proven in markets like the US and Europe. In regions where Chrome dominates and ad blocking is less common, there may be an opportunity to introduce these solutions, but the willingness to pay may be lower.

Cheapest way to test the opportunity: Create a simple landing page offering a &quot;Chrome to Firefox migration guide&quot; or a &quot;safe browsing setup service&quot; and drive traffic via social media or forums where users are complaining about the MV2 removal. If you get sign-ups or inquiries, that validates demand. For a product, you could build a minimal MV3 ad blocker and offer it for free with a donation button, then measure adoption and feedback.

Evidence that would confirm the opportunity is real: A significant number of users actively searching for &quot;best ad blocker for Chrome after MV3&quot; \(as seen in the tool results\), an increase in Firefox downloads or market share, or a surge in sign-ups for migration services. If users are willing to pay for a service that sets up safe browsing for their parents, that would confirm the consulting opportunity.

However, be cautious: the ad-blocking market is crowded, and Google&\#x27;s dominance makes it hard to challenge. The opportunity may be more about serving the migration wave than building the next uBlock Origin.

**「Risks」** The apparent opportunity to build MV3-compliant ad blockers or migration tools carries several risks. First, the technical limitations of Manifest V3 \(e.g., declarativeNetRequest with limited rule sets\) may make it difficult to match uBlock Origin&\#x27;s effectiveness, leading to user dissatisfaction and poor retention. Second, Google&\#x27;s policy could shift again, as it has delayed MV2 removal multiple times; any reversal would undermine the urgency of the opportunity. Third, regulatory scrutiny of Google&\#x27;s dominance in browsers and ad tech could force changes, but this is uncertain and slow-moving. Fourth, competition is intense: established players like AdGuard and Brave already offer MV3-compliant solutions, and Firefox remains a strong alternative for privacy-focused users, potentially fragmenting the market. Fifth, the capital and expertise required to develop a robust ad blocker or browser extension are non-trivial, and monetization is challenging—users expect ad blockers to be free, and selling user data would defeat the purpose. Finally, timing risk: if users simply migrate to Firefox or other browsers, the demand for Chrome-specific solutions may shrink. The evidence of user frustration is real, but it may not translate into willingness to pay for a new product, especially when free alternatives exist.

### [ChatGPT Ads Hits $1 Billion Annualized Revenue: A Milestone in AI Monetization](https://openai.com/index/expanding-access-to-ai-with-chatgpt-ads) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI announced that its ChatGPT Ads business has reached a $1 billion annualized revenue run rate and is expanding globally. This milestone indicates strong market demand for advertising within AI platforms and supports OpenAI&\#x27;s strategy of offering free and affordable AI access funded by ads.

rss · OpenAI Blog · Aug 31, 04:00

**「Background」** OpenAI has been exploring monetization avenues beyond subscriptions to sustain and expand access to its AI tools. Advertising is a common model in digital platforms, but its application to AI chatbots is relatively new. The $1 billion run rate suggests that advertisers see value in reaching ChatGPT&\#x27;s large user base. This development is part of a broader trend where AI companies are integrating ads to offset costs and provide free tiers, similar to how search engines and social media platforms have used ads to fund free services.

**「Opportunity」** The milestone signals a viable market for AI-native advertising. Advertisers are willing to pay to reach ChatGPT&\#x27;s users, creating demand for ad formats, targeting tools, and analytics specifically designed for conversational AI. This opens opportunities for:
\- Ad tech companies to build platforms for AI ad placement and measurement.
\- Marketing agencies to specialize in AI platform advertising strategies.
\- Content creators and publishers to explore sponsored content within AI responses.
\- Small teams could develop niche tools for ad campaign management on ChatGPT, though entry barriers include API access and compliance.
The cheapest way to test the opportunity is to offer consulting services to small businesses on how to advertise on ChatGPT, or to build a simple analytics dashboard for ad performance. Evidence of real demand would be sustained advertiser spending and the emergence of third-party tools for AI ad management.

**「Risks」** The $1 billion figure is an annualized run rate, not actual revenue, and could be based on early momentum that may not sustain. Regulatory risks include privacy concerns and potential rules on AI advertising transparency. Competition is high, as major tech companies like Google and Meta are also exploring AI ads. Capital requirements for building ad tech are significant, and timing is uncertain as the AI advertising ecosystem is nascent. The opportunity may fail if user backlash against ads in AI tools reduces engagement.

### [The Extraordinary Rise of Miami’s Economy](https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2026/08/31/the-extraordinary-rise-of-miamis-economy) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Miami&\#x27;s economy is booming, driven by an influx of capital and political factors, according to The Economist. The city&\#x27;s rapid growth raises questions about whether this success can be sustained over the long term.

rss · The Economist · Aug 31, 17:01

**「Background」** Miami&\#x27;s economy has been growing rapidly, driven by an influx of capital and political factors. The city has attracted wealthy individuals and businesses, partly due to its favorable tax environment and its position as a gateway to Latin America. In recent years, Miami has also become a hub for finance, technology, and real estate, with significant investment flowing into the region. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics provides economic data for the Miami area, and Miami-Dade County publishes an economic data snapshot, both of which track employment, wages, and other indicators. This boom is part of a broader shift in U.S. regional economic geography, as people and companies move from higher-cost, higher-tax areas to places like Florida. The sustainability of this growth is a key question, as it depends on continued capital inflows and political stability.

**「Commercial Signal」** Miami&\#x27;s economic boom, driven by capital inflows and political factors, is creating a broad set of business opportunities. The most visible demand is in real estate: luxury waterfront homes, condos in Brickell and South Beach, and investment properties are attracting both domestic and foreign buyers. This is supported by the presence of real estate firms and law firms actively marketing these opportunities, indicating a mature market with established players.

Beyond real estate, the influx of capital and people is likely to spur demand for services such as finance, legal, tech, and hospitality. However, the article does not specify which sectors are growing fastest, so the opportunity is more general.

For a small team or individual, the most accessible entry point is real estate services—such as property management, real estate brokerage, or niche consulting for foreign investors. A cheaper test would be to offer virtual services like market analysis or relocation assistance to out-of-state or foreign buyers, leveraging online platforms. Evidence that this opportunity is real would include sustained population growth, rising property values, and increasing inquiries from investors.

However, the market is competitive, with established firms already dominating. Capital requirements for direct real estate investment are high, and the boom may be cyclical. Regulatory risks include potential changes in tax policies or foreign investment rules. Timing is critical: entering at the peak could lead to losses if the market cools. Therefore, while opportunities exist, they require careful research and a clear differentiation strategy.

**「Risks」** The Miami boom carries significant risks. First, climate change poses a direct threat: rising sea levels, flooding, and extreme heat could undermine the city&\#x27;s long-term viability. A 2026 report estimates that Miami&\#x27;s hot climate already costs over $10 billion annually in lost productivity, exceeding the county&\#x27;s entire budget. Infrastructure is struggling to keep pace with population growth, and without major investment, the city&\#x27;s resilience is questionable. Second, the boom may be driven by cyclical capital inflows and political factors that could reverse, leading to a bust. Third, regulatory and insurance costs are likely to rise as climate risks materialize, potentially deterring investment. Finally, competition from other Sun Belt cities and the high cost of living could limit the sustainability of growth. Any business opportunity must account for these risks, and the apparent prosperity may not be durable.
